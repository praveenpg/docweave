Field Survey Guide
==================

This guide explains how to record a survey site. Read it *before* your first
visit; the checklist at the end is the minimum for a valid record.

Preparing equipment
-------------------

Each kit contains:

* a GNSS receiver,
* spare batteries, and

* a printed copy of the site map.

  Keep the map in the waterproof sleeve.

Recording a site
----------------

1. Stand at the marker and wait for a fix.
   The receiver shows **FIX** when it is ready.
2. Record the coordinates:
   - latitude and longitude in decimal degrees;
   - the *estimated* horizontal error.
3. Photograph the marker.

![Survey marker with scale bar](images/marker.jpg "Marker photo")

The receiver writes a log line like this:

```text
2026-10-02T09:14:07Z FIX lat=51.50135 lon=-0.14189 err=1.8m
```

> Tip: if the fix takes more than five minutes,
> move away from trees and try again.
>
> > Nested note: tall buildings cause the same problem.

Units and symbols
-----------------

Temperatures use °C, distances use metres, and the elevation symbol is Δh.
Survey teams in other regions record names in their own scripts: 東京 (Tokyo),
القاهرة (Cairo) and Ελλάδα (Greece). Use the combining form e&#769; only when
the source uses it. Team emoji such as 👩‍🔬 are allowed in comments.

line two continues the paragraph.

See the [glossary][] and the <https://example.org/survey/faq> page for help, or
write to <survey-help@example.org>. Use &amp; rather than "and" in site codes,
and &copy; notices belong in the footer.
